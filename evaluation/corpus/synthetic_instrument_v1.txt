Article 1

1. The operator shall notify the competent authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of a significant incident, where the incident has a material impact on service continuity.
2. This paragraph does not contain a normative requirement and exists only to describe the general purpose of this instrument for background context, a hard negative for detection purposes.
3. Where the operator is a manufacturer, and where the affected product has already been recalled under a separate procedure, the operator shall additionally submit a corrective action report within 30 days.
4. The operator shall notify the CSIRT within 24 hours unless the incident has already been reported to the competent authority under Article 1(1).
5. This paragraph provides examples of incidents that should be considered significant, without imposing any independent notification obligation beyond paragraph 1.

Article 2

1. The provider must maintain records of all incidents referenced in Article 1(1) for a period of five years.
2. A provider that qualifies as a microenterprise, as defined further below, is exempted from paragraph 1 unless it processes data on behalf of an essential entity.
3. This paragraph discusses definitions and contains the word "shall" only in reference to a defined term, not as an obligation: "essential entity" shall mean, for the purposes of this instrument, an entity so designated by the competent authority under a separate designation procedure described in Annex I.
4. The provider shall retain incident records in a format that is readily accessible to the competent authority upon request, except where retention is prohibited by another applicable law.
5. Records maintained under Article 2(1) shall be protected against unauthorised access using industry-standard encryption, and shall be reviewed at least annually for accuracy.

Article 3

1. The essential entity shall implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to manage the risks posed to the security of network and information systems.
2. The competent authority may issue further guidance on the measures described in Article 3(1).
3. Nothing in this Article should be read as reducing the obligations set out in Article 2(1).
4. Where the essential entity operates in more than one Member State, and where the relevant competent authorities have not agreed on a lead authority, the essential entity shall notify each competent authority separately.
5. The essential entity shall conduct a risk assessment at least annually, and shall report the results to the competent authority within 15 days of completion, unless the competent authority has waived this requirement in writing.

Article 4 (Amendment to Article 1)

1. Article 1(1) is amended to read: the operator shall notify the competent authority within 24 hours, instead of 72 hours, of becoming aware of a significant incident, where the incident has a material impact on service continuity.

Article 5

1. The important entity shall assess the cybersecurity risk posed by its direct suppliers and service providers before entering into a contractual relationship.
2. Where a supplier has previously been subject to a significant incident, the important entity shall conduct enhanced due diligence unless the supplier has since obtained independent security certification.
3. This paragraph is provided for contextual background only and does not impose any obligation on any party.
4. The important entity must require its suppliers to notify it of any significant incident affecting the supply chain within 48 hours.
5. Suppliers designated as critical under Annex II shall be subject to on-site audits at least once every two years, and shall provide audit cooperation, except where national security considerations apply.

Article 6

1. The management body of the essential entity shall approve the cybersecurity risk-management measures taken under Article 3(1).
2. Members of the management body shall follow training to gain sufficient knowledge and skills to identify risks and assess cybersecurity risk-management practices.
3. The competent authority may impose administrative fines on an essential entity that fails to comply with Article 6(1) or Article 6(2).
4. This Article does not apply to microenterprises as defined in Article 2(2).
5. Where the essential entity is part of a group, and where the parent undertaking has already approved equivalent measures at group level, the management body's approval under Article 6(1) shall be presumed unless a competent authority objects in writing within 30 days.
